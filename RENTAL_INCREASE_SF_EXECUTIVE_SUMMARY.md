## 3. How big real estate corporations vs individual owners handle increases

- **Both are bound by identical caps** for these units: the AGA, the once-per-12-months rule, the 12-month first-increase rule, the banking rules (banked only after ≥24 full months without any increase; no expiry during the tenancy; no cap; not compounded/prorated — S-04), the Rent Increase License (Housing Inventory registration — S-01, S-04), and the petition-only passthroughs (capital improvement, operating & maintenance, special circumstances, etc. — S-05).
- **Where the law distinguishes owners:** for single-family homes/condominiums, the AB 1482 exemption is lost if the owner is a REIT, corporation or LLC (§1947.12(d)(5)) — S-13. Costa-Hawkins vacancy decontrol (§1954.53) lets ANY owner set the first rent on a new tenancy — average corporate and small owners alike (S-15).
- **Market practice (labeled, not official data):** institutional/corporate owners typically apply the maximum lawful AGA each year on the anniversary date and bank unused increases systematically (disciplined compliance-calendar management, dedicated housing-inventory filings). Individual owners more often skip years (creating larger banked amounts, as apparently happened with Unit 2) and then either (a) never impose them, or (b) impose them with a 90-day notice. No official 94122 dataset on corporate-vs-individual increase behavior could be read this session; this distinction is framework + labeled general practice only (Flag F-R13).
- **Practical consequence for these units:** the "normal business practice" benchmark a tenant lawyer would measure against is the AGA/banking math itself — properly filed, properly noticed, nothing more. Both owner types must clear the same bar.

## 4. How to properly notify and reasonable reasons

**Reasonable (lawful) reasons** — cost of living via the AGA; banked unused increases (with ≥24-month rule); petition-approved amounts (capital improvements, operating & maintenance, special circumstances, utility worksheets, bond passthroughs); Costa-Hawkins vacancy reset ONLY on a new tenancy.
**Not reasonable / unlawful bases** — a new or additional roommate; retaliation for protected activity; any increase applied because of a protected class; "market rent" above the AGA during an ongoing tenancy; above-AGA increases without an approved petition retroactive to a valid notice date.

**Correct procedure (S-01, S-04, S-05, S-12):** (1) confirm Housing Inventory registration / Rent Increase License; (2) confirm the rent ledger and anniversary date; (3) compute on base rent only, no rounding up; (4) serve **30-day** written notice (90 days if the combined increase exceeds 10%), personally or by mail (+5 days); (5) notice must state dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and which portion (if any) is banked; (6) use the percentage in effect on the effective date; (7) keep proof of service; (8) first increase only 12 months after the lease began.

## 5. Would tenant-lawyer scrutiny uphold these increases?

Any proposed increase survives SF scrutiny only if it is (a) within the AGA or a petition-approved amount, (b) within banking rules, (c) correctly licensed and noticed, (d) non-retaliatory, (e) unrelated to protected class. Both units clear that bar **only as computed on the site's Units page**, and only if Unit 2's ledger confirms the $3,150 base (Flag F-R7) and Unit 1's banked portion waits for February 1, 2027 (Flag F-R5).

**Raise one or both units?** The law does not require equal increases across units; increases are per-tenancy. However, uniform, identical-methodology, well-documented AGA notices on **both** units — each computed from its own lawful base — is the lowest-risk posture: it mirrors "normal business practice," eliminates any inference of selective targeting, and cannot reasonably support a discrimination claim because both increases rest on the same objective, published formula applied uniformly. Raising only Unit 2 while skipping Unit 1 (or vice versa) is lawful if justified by each unit's own anniversary/banking history — but any fact pattern where the higher increase lands on one occupant cohort without an objective basis invites FEHA/Unruh/FHA scrutiny and a free-counsel tenant challenge. The discrimination-clean approach: same notice template, same percentage basis, same service method, both units, documented rationale — or a written, objective reason (different banking histories) retained in the file.
