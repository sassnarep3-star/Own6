## 3 · The law that governs (strictest wins)

| Layer | Rule | Result for these units |
|---|---|---|
| **Local — SF Rent Ordinance** | AGA = 60% of Bay Area CPI-U; once per 12 months; base rent only; **1.6% in effect 3/1/2026–2/28/2027** (announced 2026-01-14); banking of skipped AGAs (add, don't compound, 24-month condition, no ceiling); annual **Housing Inventory report → rent increase license** (deadline March 1) — increases without it are unenforceable; 30-day (≤10%) / 90-day (>10%) notice, +5 days if mailed | **Governs.** |
| **State — AB 1482 / Civ. Code § 1947.12** | 5% + CPI or 10%, whichever lower; max two increments per 12 months; SF Area caps 6.3% (8/1/25–7/31/26) and 8.8% (8/1/26–7/31/27); stricter local rent control excluded from the state cap (§ 1947.12(d)(3)) | Backstop only — local 1.6% is lower and controls |
| **Federal** | **No federal rent cap exists.** FHA bars discrimination in terms of rental (race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, disability); HUD Region IX (San Francisco) enforces. Notice periods: Cal. Civ. Code § 827 (≤10% → 30 days; >10% → 90 days; mail adds 5 days, CCP § 1013).
Retaliation: Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 bars any increase within 180 days of a tenant's habitability complaint.

## 4 · What is reasonable and normal (the answer)

**A uniform 1.6% increase for both units is the normal, market-of-record, tenant-lawyer-approvable
course.** It is exactly the rate the SF Rent Board publishes for 2026-27, taken once per 12 months:

| | Calculation | New base rent |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 1 | $3,995.00 × 1.6% = **$63.92** | **$4,058.92** |
| Unit 2 | $3,150.00 × 1.6% = **$50.40** | **$3,200.40** |

**Unit 2 optionality (flagged — verify first):** if — and only if — *no* lawful increase was imposed
after 2019-06-01, the AGAs skipped 2020→2025 (1.8 + 0.7 + 2.3 + 3.6 + 1.7 + 1.4 = **11.5%**, $362.25)
may be imposed as banked increases, and together with the current 1.6% (1.6% on/after the June 1
anniversary) total **13.1% = $412.65 → $3,562.65**. Requirements and caveats: 90-day notice;
banked portion itemized; license in effect; each skipped increment ≥24 full months old (the 1.4%
increment from 2025-06-01 is includable only on/after 2027-06-01); percentages added, never
compounded. **This is a documented extraordinary step, not "normal". Do not use it without written
confirmation from the SF Rent Board counselor (415-252-4600) or counsel, and a rent ledger proving
the no-increase history.** Unit 1's 2026 skipped increment (1.4%) is not usable before ~2028-02-01.

Anything above the AGA without a Rent Board petition is unlawful and would be struck on a tenant
petition; nothing here proposes that.

## 5 · One unit or both — the discrimination answer

**Raise both units with the same method, percentage and effective-date rule (recommended), or neither.**
The increments are tiny ($63.92 / $50.40 per month); different treatment saves almost nothing and
buys a discrimination/retaliation inquiry. Differential treatment across units is lawful only when
resting on documented, objective, non-protected criteria — e.g. verifiably different anniversary
histories and banking eligibility — never on protected class (FHA/FEHA/Unruh). Unit 1 in particular
just had an occupancy change (2026-04-01); targeting only Unit 1 invites a roommate-substitution
inference even if unfounded. Uniform 1.6% on both units, on/after each unit's anniversary, with
identical notice paperwork, is the structure a San Francisco tenant lawyer would approve.

## 6 · How big corporations vs individual owners behave (and what it means)

Corporate multifamily owners commonly price with algorithmic revenue management — the RealPage
record shows how (nonpublic competitor data; Sherman Act case; Nov 2025 proposed consent judgment
ending runtime use of competitors' nonpublic data). **None of that lawfully moves a SF controlled
unit above the 1.6% AGA.** Individual SF owners typically take the AGA annually or skip-and-bank
quietly; obligations (Housing Inventory, license, notice, banking rules) are identical for both.
There is no official dataset on owner-type behavior — flagged as analysis, not fact.

## 7 · How to notify — the short list

1. Confirm coverage & exemptions; call Rent Board counselor 415-252-4600 if unsure.
2. File/confirm the Housing Inventory (portal.sfrb.org; deadline March 1) → rent increase license.
3. Pull the rent ledger; confirm no increase in the last 12 months; confirm anniversary.
4. Screen for retaliation (180-day rule) — if any tenant complaint is pending, stop.
5. Draft notice: dollar amount, percentage, effective date, banked portion itemized; use the AGA
   in effect on the effective date; calculate on base rent only; never round up.
6. Serve: 30-day (≤10%) or 90-day (>10%); +5 days if mailed; personally or CCP § 1013 mail; keep proof of service.
7. Effective on the anniversary; once per 12 months thereafter.

Free tenant-side help exists and any tenant will likely have it: Rent Board counselor (free),
LawHelpCA legal-aid referral (non-official host, labeled as such), CA Civil Rights Department,
HUD Region IX (415) 489-6524, and tenant nonprofits. Assume notices will be scrutinized — write them
accordingly.

## 8 · What a tenant lawyer will check — and how this plan passes

Coverage → math → frequency → notice form/service → license → no retaliation → no discrimination →
uniform treatment. A uniform 1.6% with license + compliant notice passes cleanly. A 13.1% banked
increase passes **only** with bulletproof ledger proof, itemized notice, license and counselor/counsel
sign-off. Anything above AGA without a petition fails outright. (Full 12-item checklist: site,
"One or both units" page.)
