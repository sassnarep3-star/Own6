Strategy board

Proper scores on official outcomes, ranked by skill rather than by wins. Every value carries its sample size, a week-cluster interval and a multiplicity adjustment for the size of the frozen family. The prospective leaderboard is empty by rule: backtest numbers are barred from it, so the state of every strategy is paper.

How to read the skill column. Brier skill is 1 − (model Brier ÷ control Brier). The point estimate uses every scored game; the interval resamples whole weeks, because games in the same week share conditions. When the lower bound is below zero the honest reading is "no demonstrated skill", however good the point estimate looks.

Removal-of-luck panel

The exact Murphy decomposition of each score, computed per distinct forecast value so the identity holds (a binned version loses within-bin spread — an earlier version of this panel did exactly that and a test caught it): reliability is the part of the score caused by miscalibration, resolution is the part earned by separating good games from bad, uncertainty is the irreducible base-rate variance. Luck share is reliability ÷ Brier.

What the luck share says here. For the three probability models it is near 1.0, and that number means something more specific than "the model is lucky": their Brier score is almost entirely calibration error. Those models forecast a mean home probability near 0.55 while home clubs actually won 45.8% of the scored games. A large share of the accuracy they do have comes from the resolution term — they rank games better than a base rate — but the level of the forecast is wrong, which is a defect that a well-calibrated forecaster of the same shape would not have.

Where the miscalibration comes from, measured

The registered model adds a fixed +2.5 points of home-field advantage. In this corpus the realised mean home margin is −0.53 points over 150 games and home clubs won 47.3% of them.
