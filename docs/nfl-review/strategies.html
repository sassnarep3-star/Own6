On luck. Statistical validation can reduce the influence of luck; it cannot remove it. NIST is explicit that a 95 % confidence interval does not mean there is a 95 % probability that the interval contains the true mean, and that the confidence coefficient describes the method rather than the truth of the estimate. Every interval on this project is therefore reported with its cluster count and is empty until a verified price exists.
No profitability is claimed. The reproducible lab in the lines-lab section reports holdout returns for three strategies whose confidence intervals all include zero, computed on a community dataset with no declared licence. Those figures are labelled computations on non-official data and are not an official league or market result.
