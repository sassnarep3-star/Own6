THE BOTTOM LINE

Recommended course of action

  1. Raise both units by the same, simple method: the 1.6% Annual General Adjustment once per 12 months. Unit 1: $3,995.00 × 1.6% = $63.92 → new rent $4,058.92. Unit 2: $3,150.00 × 1.6% = $50.40 → new rent $3,200.40. Uniform treatment is the cleanest defence against discrimination claims and the easiest outcome for a tenant lawyer to approve.
  2. Unit 2 may hold large banked increases (11.5% from AGAs 2020–2025 + current 1.6% = 13.1%, i.e. $412.65 → $3,562.65) — but only if (a) no increase was imposed at any point after 2019-06-01, (b) the 24-month banking rule is satisfied increment-by-increment, (c) a rent increase license is in effect, (d) a 90-day notice itemizes the banked portion. This is a ceiling, not a recommendation — verify with the Rent Board first (flags F-02/F-06).
  3. Do not raise Unit 1 “because a new roommate joined” — unlawful under SF’s additional-occupants rule; do not treat the 2026-04-01 substitution as a vacancy and do not reset to market.
  4. Before any increase: report to the Rent Board Housing Inventory (deadline March 1) and hold a rent increase license, or the notice is unenforceable (see laws).
  5. Never use an increase as retaliation — Cal. Civ. Code § 1942.5 bars increases within 180 days of a tenant’s habitability complaint.
