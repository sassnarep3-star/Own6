DISCRIMINATION & FAIRNESS REVIEW

Should you raise one unit — or both?

Short answer: raise both units with the same method and percentage (uniform 1.6%), or raise neither. Raising only one unit can be lawful, but it is harder to defend, and the differential it creates buys almost nothing ($63.92 vs $50.40 per month).

The legal discrimination frame

SF Rent OrdinanceRent increases limited to AGA + banking mechanics; added-occupant surcharges unlawful; increases above cap only via petition.S01 · S04

A rent increase is not discriminatory because it differs across units — units can lawfully be treated differently on documented, objective, non-protected grounds (different anniversary dates, verified banking differences). It becomes unlawful when the difference tracks a protected class, or when it is retaliation, or when it exceeds the cap. The legal risk is therefore not “different math”, it is unexplained or unexplained-away difference.

The verdict

Uniform 1.6% on both units (effective on each unit’s anniversary), after Housing Inventory confirmation and with fully-compliant notices, is normal business practice for San Francisco rent-controlled housing. It is:

  • Reasonable and normal — exactly the rate the Rent Board publishes for 2026-27, at the legal ceiling, once per year [S01][S02].
  • Discrimination-proof in structure — identical method, identical timing rule, identical paperwork for comparable units; no protected-class criterion anywhere in the decision [S11][S12].
  • Tenant-lawyer-reviewable and approvable — a tenant lawyer checking coverage, math, frequency, license, notice and retaliation finds each element clean (table above).
  • Local > state > federal — SF’s stricter cap governs; the federal role is only anti-discrimination/antitrust, and both are satisfied [S07][S08][S11].

If the owner prefers the Unit 2 13.1% banked path for legitimate financial reasons, it can be lawful — but it is a documented extraordinary step, not “normal”: verify the ledger with the tenant or Rent Board, stage it on/after 2027-06-01, use a 90-day notice, and obtain written confirmation of banking eligibility from the Rent Board counselor or counsel first.
