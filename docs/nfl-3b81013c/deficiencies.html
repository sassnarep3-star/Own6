Depth deficiency

Where second, third and fourth-string players actually have to start

Unit-by-unit exposure across the Week 10 report, with the verified share stated explicitly so a depleted-looking unit is never mistaken for a measured one.

Coverage of the depth screen is 3 of 48 report slots. Only those slots can carry a numeric index. Reading the index as a complete measure of how depleted a unit is would be wrong, and the detail column says so on every row.

Unit-level depth deficiency, ranked by verified exposure

Rows group the injury-report entries by club, side of the ball and unit. The cluster multiplier illustrates the non-linear cost of losing two or three starters in one cohesive unit; because the raw index already sums independent weights, applying a multiplier compounds the interaction twice, so the multiplied column is shown for illustration and the raw exposure is the number to trust.

Why the defensive side of the ball is harder to certify than the offensive side

An offensive-line absence is visible in the starting five and is usually reported directly. A defensive rotation absence is a snap-share question: a starting cornerback who is out may be replaced by committee, with a nickel back taking the slot and a safety rotating down. Without official snap counts by player, the incoming string cannot be established, which is exactly why most defensive rows on this page are blank in the index column.
