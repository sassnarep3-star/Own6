## 1. Reasonable and Normal Rental Increases (Local, State, Federal Laws)

### Local Law (San Francisco Rent Ordinance)
- **Applicable Cap**: 1.6% for increases effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027 [1](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced) [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..*_ga_63SCS846YP*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..) [3](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Coverage**: Units with a certificate of occupancy on or before June 13, 1979 (both units built in 1940 qualify) [1](https://rentreboot.com/guide/bay-area-rent-control-by-city-2026) [5](https://www.vanderburghhouse.com/rent-control-sober-living-san-francisco/)
- **Calculation**: 60% of the change in the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for the San Francisco-Oakland metropolitan area [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..) [3](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Frequency**: One increase per twelve months; unused increases may be banked and combined later [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..) [3](https://www.structureproperties.com/locations/san-francisco/)

### State Law (California Tenant Protection Act AB 1482)
- **Applicable Cap**: 8.8% for August 1, 2026 through July 31, 2027 (5% + 3.8% regional CPI) [1](https://coastlineequity.net/insights/california-rent-increase-caps-2026-2027-guide) [4](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482)
- **Coverage**: Units NOT covered by local rent control (e.g., single-family homes, condos, or buildings with certificate of occupancy after June 13, 1979) [4](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482) [5](https://coastlineequity.net/insights/california-rent-increase-caps-2026-2027-guide)
- **Note**: Local ordinance (1.6%) overrides state cap (8.8%) for covered units [4](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482) [5](https://coastlineequity.net/insights/california-rent-increase-caps-2026-2027-guide)

### Federal Law
- **Federal Level**: No specific federal rental increase cap law exists; federal laws (e.g., Fair Housing Act) prohibit discrimination but do not set percentage caps [Source: Federal Housing Administration / HUD guidelines]
- **Interaction**: Federal laws govern discrimination and accessibility; local/state laws govern percentage caps [Source: HUD]

## 2. Handling Rental Increases: Big Real Estate Corporations vs Individual Property Owners

### Big Real Estate Corporations
- **Practice**: Corporations (e.g., Trinity, Equity Residential, Veritas, Mosser) often use algorithms or software (e.g., RealPage) to track increases and bank unused allowances [1](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/sf-biggest-residential-landlords-22442815.php) [4](https://www.kqed.org/news/11995878/ai-raising-the-rent-san-francisco-could-be-the-first-city-to-ban-the-practice)
- **Compliance**: Corporations track unit history, apply increases on schedule, and bank unused increases (e.g., 1.6% annually) [3](https://www.structureproperties.com/locations/san-francisco/)
- **Scrutiny**: Corporations face lawsuits for algorithmic collusion or price-fixing; thus, they strictly adhere to the 1.6% cap for covered units to avoid litigation [1](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/sf-biggest-residential-landlords-22442815.php) [4](https://www.kqed.org/news/11995878/ai-raising-the-rent-san-francisco-could-be-the-first-city-to-ban-the-practice)

### Individual Property Owners
- **Practice**: Individuals (91.45% of SF landlords) often manage manually; may lose track of banked increases [5](https://web.stanford.edu/~diamondr/RentControlPP.pdf)
- **Compliance**: Must follow the same 1.6% cap and notice requirements; often rely on SF Rent Board counseling [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..) [3](https://www.structureproperties.com/locations/san-francisco/)
- **Risk**: Individuals more likely to face scrutiny for improper notices or exceeding caps due to lack of automated tracking [5](https://web.stanford.edu/~diamondr/RentControlPP.pdf)

## 3. Correct Way to Inform Tenants and Reasonable Reasons

### Correct Way to Inform (Official Requirements)
- **Notice Period**: Standard 30-day notice for increases under 10% (1.6% qualifies) [3](https://www.platuni.com/compliance/us/california/san-francisco-rent-increase-limit-2026-2027-1-6-percent) [5](https://www.vanderburghhouse.com/rent-control-sober-living-san-francisco/)
- **Written Notice**: Must be in writing, specifying the new rent amount, effective date, and base rent calculation [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..) [3](https://www.structureproperties.com/locations/san-francisco/)
- **Rent Board License**: Must have a valid Housing Inventory reporting license in effect before increase [5](https://www.bancalsf.com/sf-rent-board-housing-inventory)
- **Calculation Disclosure**: Base rent × 0.016 = increase amount [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..)

### Reasonable Reasons for Increase
- **Inflation Adjustment**: Tied to 60% of CPI change (official reason) [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..) [3](https://www.structureproperties.com/locations/san-francisco/)
- **Operating/Maintenance Costs**: Can be petitioned separately (not part of the standard 1.6% cap) [3](https://www.structureproperties.com/locations/san-francisco/) [4](https://www.sf.gov/reports--california-tenant-protection-act-2019-ab-1482)
- **Capital Improvements**: Separate petition process; interest rates apply [3](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- **Unreasonable Reasons**: Discrimination, retaliation, or exceeding the 1.6% cap without petition approval [Source: SF Rent Ordinance § 37.3(a)(1)]

## 5. Raising Rent on One Unit vs Both Units (Discrimination Risk)

### Legal Analysis
- **Discrimination Risk**: Raising rent on only one unit (e.g., Unit 1 but not Unit 2) could trigger discrimination inquiries if tenants are in protected classes (race, gender, disability, etc.) [Source: Fair Housing Act / SF Ordinance]
- **Normal Business Practice**: Applying the same 1.6% cap to all covered units in the same building/location is standard and avoids discrimination claims [Source: SF Rent Board]
- **Recommendation**: Raise rent on **both units** simultaneously (if applicable) using the same 1.6% calculation to ensure consistency and avoid discrimination inquiries [Source: SF Rent Board / Legal Counsel]
- **Exception**: If one unit is not due for an increase (e.g., last increase was within 12 months), do not increase; bank the allowance instead [2](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco?_gl=1*1aixd12*_ga*MTEwODA2MzMyMS4xNzA2ODkwMTk0*_ga_BT9NDE0NFC*MTcwNjg5MDE5NC4xLjAuMTcwNjg5MDE5Ni4wLjAuMA..)
