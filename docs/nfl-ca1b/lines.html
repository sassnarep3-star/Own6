The probability multiplier k = 1.8 is the median value selected walk-forward on data strictly older than each prediction, and it is applied here unchanged.

Read the spread as negative for the home favourite. Spread and total are rounded to the half point, which is how a line is quoted; the unrounded model numbers are in the CSV next to them. American odds are the fair (no-vig) equivalent of the published probability, shown for comparison only. Every market cell is blank because no official NFL page publishes a price.
Why the probability does not match the spread. The spread comes from the margin equation. The probability deliberately widens the margin variance by the walk-forward factor k, because the raw model was shown to be overconfident in its tail. So a 9.5-point fair spread legitimately pairs with a 69% probability here: the spread is the point estimate, the probability is the calibrated one. Using the spread to imply the probability would reproduce exactly the overconfidence this pack measured.
