CATEGORY 03 · JOINT OUTCOMES

Correlation & combinations

Correlation is an empirical property of defined outcomes in a defined context. No correlation values are asserted here: none can be calculated without historical paired observations and a market/selection definition.

Current verified correlation values: blank. There is no collected NBA outcome-pair dataset, player lineup dataset, or timestamped combo market history in this repository.

How to estimate it

  1. Specify the exact random variables and population, e.g., two player stat outcomes within the same game, with season/date range and inclusion rules.
  2. Join observations by official game identifier and player; resolve trades, DNPs, overtime, void/settlement rules, and changing roles before calculating.
  3. Report Pearson correlation for linear association and Spearman rank correlation as a robustness view. Give paired sample count, confidence interval (game-cluster bootstrap recommended), and stratified results.
  4. For threshold bets, estimate joint event probability directly: P(A and B). Correlation of raw stats does not uniquely determine the joint probability of overs, especially for non-normal, discrete or truncated outcomes.
  5. Evaluate out of sample and within comparable role/availability/market conditions. Do not select only the most correlated pairs from the same validation period.

Parlay pricing

For legs with estimated marginal probabilities, do not multiply them as if independent unless independence is justified for the particular events. Prefer a calibrated joint distribution that reflects shared pace, minutes, lineup, game script, and overlapping stat production. Price the joint event against the offered combo odds, including book rules and any correlation adjustment. Require the same data, timing, and out-of-sample gates used for single bets.
