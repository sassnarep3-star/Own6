Backtest and settlement

Singles, parlays, same-game parlays and prop combos, settled against official final scores.

Requirement 2 exists because without a results feed a backtest is a story. There are 150 graded tickets and 166 settled legs here, and every leg carries the URL of the page its score came from.

Read the price basis before the units. There is no book in this environment, so side and total legs settle at even money against our own published number and moneyline legs at our own no-vig price. A perfectly fair model is therefore expected to break even against these prices by construction: what this table measures is calibration, and anything that looks like profit is a sample artefact. That sentence is the whole reason the leaderboard ranks the signal block, not this one.

Settlement conventions

  • A push on a side or a total returns the stake: 0 units, and the leg is excluded from hit-rate and Brier denominators rather than counted as a miss. 4 push legs exist in this window.
  • A push on a moneyline returns the stake; a tie is possible and priced - the tie probability on the published Washington at New York line was 2.73%.
  • A two-leg ticket with one push pays the surviving leg's price; both push, the ticket pushes; any leg loses, the ticket loses.
  • These are declared conventions, not an operator's rules. Suggestion D966-S03 asks for a decision on push handling before any real market exists.
