Signal design

Candidate inputs to test, never presumed profitable. NBA glossary links define NBA measures; feature selection and model results remain unverified until evaluated.

Principle: use only information available at the forecast timestamp. Freeze feature definitions and transformations; retain the raw values, source, timestamp, and missingness reason.

Candidate market families

  • Single-game sides/totals: predict team points and margin distributions; compare probabilities for spread, moneyline, and total to available prices.
  • Player props: forecast opportunity then stat distribution for points, rebounds, assists, three-pointers, and combinations. Include minutes uncertainty and official availability.
  • Same-game combos/parlays: derive joint outcomes from a joint model or observed conditional dependence; price the exact combination, not the product of marginal probabilities unless independence is justified.

Proposed model stack (not a measured result)

  1. Transparent baseline: historical/team-player rates with recency weighting and regression to an appropriate league/role baseline.
  2. Opportunity layer: minutes and role distribution, conditioned only on timestamp-valid information.
  3. Outcome layer: count/continuous distributions chosen and checked against residuals, overdispersion, and calibration.
  4. Context layer: opponent and pace adjustments with regularization; retain a no-context baseline for ablation.
  5. Uncertainty layer: ensemble or resampling intervals, plus sensitivity to lineup and data uncertainty.
These are research hypotheses, not proven predictive signals. The repository contains no trained model, coefficients, feature importance, player forecasts, or measured edge.
