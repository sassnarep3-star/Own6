Parlays and prop combinations

What an n-leg ticket costs in evidence, and how many paired observations are needed before a correlation can be measured at all.

Every figure on this page is arithmetic from the formulas in src/own6/power.py: a statement of how much evidence would be needed. No NBA result, sportsbook price, correlation estimate or return is observed, scraped or inferred here. Values that cannot be confirmed stay blank and are marked unverified; a blank is not zero.

Parlays: the edge compounds, and so does the variance

Each extra leg multiplies both the payout and the joint probability. With an equal assumed 2% true edge per leg at -110, the ROI per ticket rises with the number of legs, but the variance rises faster, so the number of graded tickets needed stays in the same large range — and the independence assumption is exactly what a same-game parlay violates.

How much data a correlation estimate needs

Prop combinations and parlays are only priced correctly if dependence is measured, and measuring dependence itself needs a large paired sample. These are the paired-observation requirements for a two-sided Fisher-z test at alpha 0.05 with 80% power.

A correlation of 0.10 needs about 783 paired observations; 0.20 needs about 194. Any teammate or same-game dependence asserted from a handful of games — several earlier pages in this repository use n = 6 — is not measurable at these settings, which is why the published correlation fields remain blank and flagged.

Consequence for prop-combination strategies

Multiply-the-marginals pricing is only valid under independence. Because the dependence measurement needs hundreds of paired observations and no verified dataset is configured, the prop-combination strategies on this site stay unqualified rather than being priced on an assumption.
