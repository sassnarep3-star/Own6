Multiple testing and verdicts

Corrections for searching many strategies, and the two-condition rule that decides whether a record exceeds luck.

Every figure on this page is arithmetic from the formulas in src/own6/power.py: a statement of how much evidence would be needed. No NBA result, sportsbook price, correlation estimate or return is observed, scraped or inferred here. Values that cannot be confirmed stay blank and are marked unverified; a blank is not zero.

The leaderboard is a multiple-testing problem

Selecting the best of many candidates inflates the winner's apparent skill. Two corrections are reported: Bonferroni controls the chance of any false discovery and is the conservative gate; Benjamini-Hochberg controls the expected proportion of false discoveries and is reported as a comparison.

The rule applied to observed records

The decision rule requires both a p-value below alpha and a Wilson lower bound above the price's break-even probability. Requiring both prevents a significant but imprecise record from being called skill. Illustrative records only — no real strategy record exists on this site.

What would earn a leaderboard place

A candidate qualifies only if, after the multiple-testing correction and on out-of-sample data, its lower confidence bound still clears break-even and its calibration passes. On the published evidence, zero candidates qualify; the reward table stays empty by design rather than by omission.
