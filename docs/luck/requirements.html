Sample-size requirements

Closed-form minimum sample sizes for detecting a positive per-bet ROI, and the smallest ROI a record of a given size can resolve.

Every figure on this page is arithmetic from the formulas in src/own6/power.py: a statement of how much evidence would be needed. No NBA result, sportsbook price, correlation estimate or return is observed, scraped or inferred here. Values that cannot be confirmed stay blank and are marked unverified; a blank is not zero.

Bets required to detect a real edge

The requirement is a one-sided test at alpha 0.05 with 80% power. The last column applies a Bonferroni correction for searching 200 candidate strategies, which is the realistic cost of running a leaderboard.

Read the table as a gate, not a target. A strategy with a genuine 3% edge at -110 needs roughly 6,200 graded bets to be distinguishable from luck at these settings, and roughly 18,800 once 200 candidates compete. A 200-bet trial cannot support any claim about a 3% edge.

The resolution limit of a small record

Inverting the same formula gives the smallest ROI that a given number of bets can distinguish from noise. This is the honest resolution of the leaderboard: below these thresholds the ranking is a ranking of luck.

At 100 bets nothing under a 23.7% ROI is separable; at 1,000 bets the floor is still 7.5%. A leaderboard that rewards a 5% edge from a few hundred bets is rewarding noise.

How this connects to the validation gates

The wider project requires out-of-sample testing, calibration and uncertainty bounds before any strategy qualifies. This page supplies the sample-size input to those gates: it states the minimum n at which a gate can be evaluated at all. The published leaderboard still contains zero qualified strategies, because no authorised observation ledger exists.
