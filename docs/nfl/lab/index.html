How to use this section

1. Start with the decision, not the table

Every page that changes a decision (lines, strategies, leaderboard) opens with the gate that would have to pass for the number to matter. The Questions page lists what is unknown and what would resolve it; the Suggestions page ranks what to build next.

2. Trust the blanks

Where a market price is not licensed and verified, the cell is empty. No row is filled to look complete. The Flagged for Review page lists each blank with the source that would settle it.

3. The model is allowed to lose

The 2025 holdout is published with its losses: spread bets -1.2% (interval -12.3% to +10.4%), totals -3.7%, moneyline dogs -7.5%. That is the point. A method that cannot show its failures cannot show skill.

4. Combos are priced from measured dependence

The Correlation page reports phi and a clustered interval for same-game cover+over, and the observed-vs-independent multiplier. Independence is never assumed silently.

Executive summary

  • The signal fails. A transparent, opponent-adjusted efficiency rating does not beat the closing spread on a season it never saw. Only the downside is statistically distinguishable from zero.
  • The data is honest but thin. 89 official-value checks pass against verbatim NFL.com reads; the historical backtest leans on a community file with no licence (F-N02).
  • Most of the combo space is data-blocked, not model-blocked. 3 of 8 candidates can be tested at all; props, opening lines and historical injuries are missing.
  • The path forward is a capture problem. Licensed prices, player props, daily depth charts and dated injury archives are the four blockers. Until they exist, no strategy can qualify.
