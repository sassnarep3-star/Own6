Correlation - what may be multiplied

Whether a combo can be priced by multiplying its legs is an empirical question, not a house rule. This page answers it with 2,792 historical games, a clustered interval over 570 slate dates, and a Fisher exact test on the paired 2x2 table.

Pricing a two-leg combo three ways

Example: two legs with marginal probabilities 52% and 55%, priced at 2.61 and 2.70 decimal (the same as +161 and +170). The table shows what the payout must clear under each assumption.

The Frechet bounds show the honest range: for these two marginals the joint can be anywhere from 7% to 52% without any dependence at all. A combo price that looks generous under independence can be a guaranteed loser under the lower bound. No copula value is shown because no measured rho exists for this pair.

Rules the lab applies to every combo

  • Never multiply without a test. Independence is a labelled special case, not a default.
  • Paired residuals, not marginal rates. The unit of observation is the game, and both legs must be present.
  • Cluster by slate date. Multiple bets on one night are not independent draws from the season.
  • Publish the ratio. Observed joint divided by independent joint appears next to every price.
  • Require the sample. Gate G09 blocks any combo without 30 paired observations and a clustered interval.

What is still unknown

Player-prop dependence - the kind that actually moves same-game parlays - is untested because no prop outcomes were obtained (Q08). The only published prop conclusion is that it cannot be computed yet.
