DECISION BRIEF · 02 NOV 2026

Executive summary

What is established, how to build a selective NBA pricing process, and what remains unverified.

Bottom line: no strategy is yet proven profitable. This repository had no dataset, historical closing prices, bet outcomes, or executable model at review start. It would be misleading to provide picks, hit rates, edge estimates, correlation coefficients, or leaderboard winners.

Objective

Estimate fair outcome probabilities and prices for NBA game markets and player props, then rank candidate strategies only after point-in-time data and out-of-sample tests establish a repeatable advantage net of the actual offered price and costs. Use NBA official statistics and injury reports as inputs, not as evidence of betting profitability.

Recommended approach

  1. Build an auditable data foundation. Store timestamped NBA game/player observations, official injury reports, and independently sourced, legally obtained sportsbook offers and settlement rules. Record source, retrieval time, market, line, odds, and corrections.
  2. Model distributions, not just averages. Forecast minutes and opportunity first, then points/rebounds/assists (and joint outcomes) conditional on role, opponent, pace, lineup availability, and game state. Preserve uncertainty and test calibration.
  3. Price the market. Convert the offered odds to implied probability, account for both sides / overround, and compare with a calibrated model probability. Pass unless the estimated advantage exceeds an uncertainty and cost buffer set before testing.
  4. Validate chronologically. Use rolling-origin out-of-sample seasons or dates, prohibit future information, correct for multiple strategy searches, and track closing-line movement and realized net returns.
  5. Promote by evidence tier. Only strategies that pass every gate enter the reward leaderboard. Report sample size, confidence interval, drawdown, calibration, and market coverage; freeze definitions before a scored period.

Deliverable status

The current deliverable is a framework, not an automated live betting system. It does not collect odds, place wagers, assert that luck can be removed, or provide financial advice. Randomness cannot be eliminated; statistical uncertainty can only be measured and controlled. Definitions of thresholds are proposed controls and require calibration on a representative historical dataset before deployment.
