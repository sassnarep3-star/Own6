Why the counts and stamps matter more than the chart

  • Arizona publishes a usable chart and no date. No publication date is rendered anywhere on the page, so a freshness alarm cannot be built from it. The chart's content is still the most consequential read of the day (Beck at QB1; Brissett absent) — but the register has to record the date state, not just the players.
  • Green Bay publishes a date and says it does not trust it. The footer reads "Unofficial, Nov. 10, 2026" — nine days old at read time, unchanged since the morning read, and self-labelled unofficial. The jersey-number prefix on every cell is the case the parsing fix exists for: the register now stores the raw cell text and the stripped name side by side, so the number is preserved as evidence without ever becoming a ranking. That is the line the brief's rejection draws — depth order must never be derived from jersey number, experience or salary.
  • Houston publishes an image. Third consecutive session with zero text rows. It is not a parser defect: there is nothing to parse. Any Texans depth row therefore stays blank-and-flagged, and the register says so rather than eyeballing an image into fake certainty. The same page's gameday banner is also where the Reliant/NRG naming inconsistency showed up a second time this week (see captures).
Shared and empty slots are reproduced exactly as printed: Arizona lists Zaven Collins and Baron Browning in one OLB cell (a shared slot, not two rankings), Green Bay prints Kaleb Johnson under a blank position cell and repeats Kitan Oladapo in both safety rows. Splitting or de-duplicating those rows would manufacture a depth order that the club did not publish.
