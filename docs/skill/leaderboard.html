Leaderboard and rewards

Eligibility, scoring, and the anti-gaming properties of the ranking.

LEADERBOARD

A leaderboard must rank the worst plausible version of a strategy, not its best observed run. Otherwise it ranks luck, and it does so most reliably in exactly the situations where a real edge is hardest to see.

Scoring rule

A strategy appears on the board only if it passes all twelve gates. Eligible strategies are ranked by the lower bound of the 95% block-bootstrap interval on return per bet, with a tie-break on the deflated Sharpe. A strategy with a high point estimate and a wide interval therefore ranks below a modest strategy with a tight one — by design.

Rewards. No reward is configured or paid. Any reward scheme depends on gate SG01 (rights and legal review), which is open. Publishing a reward schedule before that gate closes would be an invitation to manufacture records that cannot be lawfully checked.

What a real entrant would have to submit

  1. A frozen plan hash committed before the first bet (SG03).
  2. A per-bet ledger with a price, a timestamp and a source for every observation (SG02, SG12).
  3. Paired model and market probabilities on the same games (SG06).
  4. The full family of strategies tried, not just the survivor (SG09).
  5. A measured correlation with n and an interval for any multi-leg bet (SG11).

Anything less is a description of a record, not evidence of skill.

Anti-gaming properties

  • Cannot win on sample size alone: SG05 requires power for the claimed edge, so padding n with uninformative bets does not help.
  • Cannot hide failed variants: SG09 rejects a family size that does not match the number of p-values supplied.
  • Cannot win on a short heater: the lower bound, not the mean, is the score.
  • Cannot win on a lucky parlay: SG11 requires dependence to be stated.
