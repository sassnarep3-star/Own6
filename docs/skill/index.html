Skill Lab — luck removal and selective skill

Originating NBA lines is easy to fake. This section is the part that is hard to fake.

LUCK REMOVAL · SELECTIVE SKILL

A strategy is only interesting if its record cannot be reproduced by a coin flip. This section builds the machinery that decides that, proves the machinery works on simulated data where the answer is known, and then applies it to everything the repository can legitimately measure. It finds no qualified strategy. That is the finding.

Executive summary

1. You cannot out-sample luck. At the standard −110 price a bettor needs 52.38% to break even. To distinguish a genuine 3% return from noise at 80% power requires about 7,900 settled bets. The repository contains 15 graded bets on one slate and a 92-game result log with no attached prices. Every ROI in it is therefore a description, not evidence.

2. A leaderboard without correction rewards the maximum of noise. With 50 zero-edge strategies of 100 bets each, the median best observed ROI is 20.3%. That is not a typo: a competition that crowns the top of fifty coin-flippers will routinely crown a 20% return. The fix is not a better filter, it is a corrected family-wide test plus a deflated Sharpe, both implemented here.

3. The repository's own correlation values mostly do not survive. 2 of 3 published correlations reproduce exactly. The rest-based value does not (-0.0162 published vs -0.0801 recomputed). The same-date value reproduces arithmetically but collapses once whole slates are resampled: the date-cluster interval is -0.0749 to 0.2205, which contains zero. Its nominal p-value was an artefact of treating non-independent pairs as independent.

4. Parlays are a dependence problem before they are a pricing problem. Two legs at 55% each and a true ρ of 0.35 have a joint probability of 35.86% against the 30.25% that independence implies. On the simulated match 21.87% is returned. A market priced at the independent product gives up most of that value to the bettor; the same model applied to negatively correlated legs — two teammates competing for one possession pool — invents an edge that does not exist. Getting the sign and the magnitude wrong is the single largest source of fictitious parlay edge.

5. No strategy qualifies, and the gates are not the obstacle. The simulated skill archetype carrying a complete evidence bundle passes all twelve gates (12/12). The archetypes without real skill fail. What blocks a real strategy is not the statistics, it is that no lawfully usable price history exists in this repository.

How to read this section

Each row on every page carries a provenance tag: SYNTHETIC (a fixed-seed simulation that describes no real game), REPO (recomputed from a named file in this repository), FORMULA (closed-form arithmetic), or SOURCE (quoted from an official page, with the URL). A row that has no verified value has a blank cell and a visible flag. Blank never means zero.
