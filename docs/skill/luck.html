Luck removal

Sample size, multiple testing, selection bias, deflated Sharpe, reality checks and PBO.

LUCK REMOVAL

Six independent ways a record can be luck, and the control that removes each. Every number on this page is produced by the build from a fixed seed or from a named file.

1. Sample size — the floor under everything else

A 3% edge at −110 needs about 7,900 settled bets for an 80%-power test. At 2% it needs roughly 17,800. This is not a matter of discipline; it is arithmetic. A 200-bet record with a 6% ROI is genuinely uninformative, and no amount of good process changes that.

2. Multiple testing — the more you try, the more you "find"

Probability of at least one false positive when testing k independent strategies at α = 0.05: 1 − (1 − α)^k.

3. Selection bias — the maximum of noise

Simulate 50 strategies with zero edge, 100 bets each, and record the best ROI. The median best is 20.3% (5th–95th percentile 14.5% to 29.8%). In 300 simulated competitions, the winner exceeded 5% in 100% of them — with no skill anywhere in the pool.

The closed-form check agrees: the median of the maximum of N independent zero-mean normals is (sd/√n)·Φ⁻¹(0.5^(1/N)) = 21.01%. A leaderboard that simply sorts by ROI is measuring this quantity.

4. Family-wise reality check

The same 60 zero-edge strategies, tested against the null that no strategy has positive expected return. The reality check uses the maximum statistic; SPA standardises each strategy and trims clearly bad ones. Both should fail to reject — there is nothing to find.

5. Deflated Sharpe

A Sharpe ratio computed after trying many variants must be deflated for the number of trials. The build reports the deflated Sharpe for every strategy that reaches SG10 and requires 0.95. In the known-answer experiment, the true-edge strategy with a single trial scores above the threshold and the zero-edge heater does not.

6. Probability of backtest overfitting on the repository's own data

Not computed, and that is the correct answer. Only 15 graded bets exist; CSCV requires >= 32 observations and >= 2 strategy series to estimate out-of-sample rank stability. No PBO value is published rather than a meaningless one. Publishing a PBO from 15 observations would produce a number with no meaning, and a meaningless number in a table is worse than a blank.

What luck removal does not do

These controls reduce the chance of publishing noise. They cannot create an edge, cannot rescue an underpowered record, and cannot substitute for a lawful dataset. A strategy that fails SG05 does not become qualified by surviving every other gate.
