Parlays and player-prop combos

Joint probability, measured dependence, and the cost of assuming independence.

PARLAYS & PROP COMBOS

A parlay is not k independent bets with a bigger payout. It is one bet on a joint event. Getting the dependence wrong is the single largest source of fake edge in parlay and player-prop-combo pricing.

Sensitivity to the correlation

Two legs at 55% each. As ρ rises, the joint probability rises and the fair price shortens (a shorter price means a smaller payout). A model that multiplies marginals therefore understates the joint probability of a positively correlated "both win" combo.

Which way the error runs

For a same-game "both legs win" combo whose legs are positively correlated (two overs on the same fast-paced game, for instance), the true joint probability is higher than the product of the marginals. The book, pricing at the product, is offering a shorter payout than the true probability justifies — so the bettor who assumes independence systematically understates their own win probability and passes on value that is really there.

The error runs the other way for negatively correlated legs, which is the usual case for two teammates' counting stats: a big scoring night for one competes with the other for the same possessions and shot attempts. There the true joint is lower than the product, and a bettor who multiplies marginals will believe they have an edge on a combo that is actually overpriced. Both directions are modelled by the same copula; only the sign of ρ changes.

In absolute terms the error is material: two legs at 55% with ρ = 0.35 give a joint of 35.86% against 30.25% assumed — a difference of 5.61 percentage points, roughly a 19% relative error, on a bet type where a 5% pricing edge is considered large.

Prop combos: the same problem, worse

Two teammates' scoring totals are plausibly negatively correlated: possessions and shot attempts are finite, so a big night for one can cost the other. The repository's single measured teammate pair (teammate_PTS_Mitchell_Allen) gives r = -0.4222 with n = 6, sourced from an ESPN game log rather than an official NBA file. At that sample size the 95% interval spans almost the entire range, so the sign is not established, let alone the magnitude. It is published here as a flagged observation, not as a usable ρ.

What the platform must require

Gate SG11 refuses to qualify any multi-leg strategy whose joint probability was produced by multiplying marginals unless independence is explicitly labelled as an assumption, and it requires a measured ρ with n and a confidence interval otherwise. The simulated correlated-parlay archetype fails SG11 when it assumes independence and passes when it carries the measured ρ — see Proof of discrimination.
