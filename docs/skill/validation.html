Proof of discrimination

Known-answer experiments: what the gates accept, what they reject, and why.

PROOF OF DISCRIMINATION

A gate that rejects everything is not a test. A gate that accepts everything is not a test either. The only way to know which one you have built is to run it against data where the answer is already known.

Known-answer experiments

Six archetypes with a known generating process. All are SYNTHETIC: seeded simulations that describe no real team, player, game or market. The seeds are fixed, so every number here is reproducible by re-running the build.

What each archetype proves

F — full evidence, real 5% edge. Passes all twelve gates, including SG05's requirement that the sample be large enough to detect the edge it claims. This is the pass path; without it the other results would only prove the gates are strict, not that they are correct.

B — real 3% edge, 3,000 bets. Passes SG07 (its return interval excludes zero) but fails SG05 and SG06. That is not a false negative; it is the power finding made concrete. Three thousand bets is not enough to detect a 3% edge with confidence, even when the edge is genuinely there.

A — zero true edge, lucky 150-bet draw (+12.0% ROI). Fails SG07, SG10 and SG05. This is the archetype a naive leaderboard would rank first. Its ROI is real, its edge is not.

E — real 2% edge, 40 bets. Fails SG05 and SG07. A genuine edge observed at a sample size that cannot support it is indistinguishable from luck, and the gates treat it that way.

C — best of 60 zero-edge strategies. In-sample ROI of 11.68% versus out-of-sample -0.73%. The collapse is not a modelling error; it is what selection does.

D — parlay priced as independent with a true ρ of 0.35. The independence assumption reports an EV of -0.00%; the realised return is 21.87%. The dependence is the entire result.

Regression protection

The same assertions are encoded in tests/test_skilllab.py. If a future change makes a gate accept the lucky or low-power archetype, or reject the full-evidence skill archetype, the test suite fails. Known-answer testing is what keeps a validation framework honest as it evolves.
