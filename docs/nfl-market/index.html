NFL Own-Line Desk

This desk originates its own NFL numbers, records a full price-snapshot store for every market it originates, grades itself against a verified results feed, and reports signal and staking as two separate scores so a lucky staking run cannot disguise a broken signal. It is built to fail closed.

The six findings that matter

  1. The NFL publishes no odds or line page. https://www.nfl.com/odds/ returns the NFL 404 page. There is therefore no official opening price, closing price, or market line to compare against.
  2. Results are verifiable, so ROI is computable in principle. 137 final scores were transcribed from nfl.com schedule pages, one URL per week.
  3. The side model is worse than a coin flip. Walking forward over weeks 5-9 it went 28-44 (hit rate 0.3889). That is a finding, not a bug: it is published, not hidden.
  4. The moneyline is the only family with a signal-shaped p-value, and it has no price. p = 0.0284 at n = 71. With no moneyline price, no ROI can be attached, so the family reports a blank staking score.
  5. Same-game parlay legs are negatively correlated in this sample. r = -0.1811 (n = 72) between the side and total grades, which means two-leg same-game parlays are *worse* than the independence assumption predicts, not better.
  6. No strategy is live and none may be. 6 of 10 gates are closed, including the forward-sample gate, which cannot be opened by any amount of analysis.
Read this before any table. The market-line and difference columns are blank for every row. That is not an unfinished section: no official NFL line exists, and filling those columns from a sportsbook would break the rule that every value traces to an official source. The consequence is that no edge has been measured, and the leaderboard ranks signal quality only.

What is in each section

  • Executive summary — the one-page read.
  • Originated lines — side first, then total, then moneyline, with fair line / market line / difference as three separate columns.
  • Price snapshots — the opening and closing store, its schema, and its coverage gap.
  • Verified results — 137 final scores with one official URL each.
  • Leaderboard — signal score and staking score kept apart.
  • Validation gates and demotion gate — how a strategy would be promoted, and how it is sent back.
  • Correlations — only from officially sourced numbers, with n on every row.
  • Depth & injuries — depth of the replacement, not just the fact of one.
  • Decision questions — what must be answered before any change is made.
  • Suggestions — options to review and choose between.
  • Sources, flagged, and repo review.
