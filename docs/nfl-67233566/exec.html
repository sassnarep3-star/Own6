Executive summary

The short version, written for a decision rather than for the record.

Position. The results problem is solved and provably so: 145 finished games reconcile 220-for-220 against two independent renderings of the official numbers. The pricing problem is not a modelling problem: no official page in the evidence set prints a price, so the fair column is real, the market column is blank, and the difference — the only thing that turns a forecast into an edge — cannot be computed at any effort level.

What the model says, honestly

  • The estimated home effect is negative (-0.4950 points). Any feature that assumes home advantage is loading a prior this season’s data does not support.
  • On the 129 graded fixtures, mean absolute error is 9.7907 points on the side and 11.3643 (T1) / 11.2093 (T2) on the total — roughly three field goals either way. That is the size of the signal this model resolves today, not an edge: which is why both totals are published, why neither has won, and why no pick language appears anywhere in this pack.
  • On the signal division, 4 of 9 graded entrants clear every gate at once — positive benchmark-relative skill, a game-cluster lower bound above zero, a calibration gap inside the pre-registered band, and survival of the multiplicity adjustment. On the staking division, 0 of 12 can qualify, and no number of extra strategies changes that: there is no price to attach a stake to, so the second stage cannot open in either direction.
  • The luck panel is unflattering on purpose: with 9 entrants at this sample size, a best-of-ten record well above a coin is the expected result of noise, which is why skill here must beat a game-cluster lower bound and not merely a point estimate.
