Executive summary

The pack in one page: what is built, what it concludes, and what needs your decision.

Bottom line

The machinery you asked for is built and verified. The verdict it returns is that no strategy is certifiable yet, and it says so in public rather than producing a leaderboard that looks like progress. That is the single most important finding in this pack: with 137 verified games (scores only) and one settled priced game, any "top strategy" would be noise wearing a rank.

What exists now

  • A results feed that checks itself. 137 finals across weeks 1–10; all 32 teams' wins, losses, ties, points for and points against recomputed from the game rows and matched against official standings — 32/32 on all five fields. Any mismatch fails the build.
  • A price store with real opens and closes. 14 games, 42 markets, 168 snapshots, each with the price as well as the line, and each marked final or not-final. Thirteen of fourteen closes are not final because the games had not kicked off.
  • The origination order as a gate. Side, then total, then moneyline — enforced by a sort in the builder so an edit cannot reorder it.
  • Fair line, market line and difference as three columns on all 42 originated markets, with the difference signed from the home side.
  • Signal separated from staking, with a published mask test. Zero certified strategies; seven blocked by named gates.
  • Replacement depth, not replacement fact. 46 depth slots across two clubs, each resolving to a named player and his string, plus a contingency column for questionable starters. New England's running back chain resolves to the third string because the second is Out.
  • Correlations with n. Three measured (n = 137, 274, 137), two uncomputable for stated reasons, one insufficient. No value inferred.
  • A demotion gate with six rules, fixed thresholds, stickiness and a re-entry bar.

The findings that matter

  1. The market and the model disagree almost everywhere, and that is not an edge. On the Week 10 side market the model is 1–3.5 points off the close on most games. A naive points-per-game rating disagreeing with a closing line is evidence about the rating, not about the line.
  2. Side/total correlation is not established. r = −0.161 at n = 137 (p = 0.061) with an interval spanning zero. Same-game parlays should therefore be priced at independence for now — with the explicit caveat that a real effect this size would need roughly 400+ games to resolve.
  3. Offence and defence are separable. r = 0.075 between points scored and points allowed in the same team-game. This is a useful negative: it justifies modelling and pricing them separately.
  4. Rest differential is the most-cited signal with the least verified data. It is uncomputable here rather than assumed.
  5. Depth charts are unofficial by the clubs' own admission. Every depth-derived row says so in the club's own words.
  6. One-week injury reports hide the case that matters. The contingency column exposes starters who are questionable in front of backups who are out.

What would change the picture

  • Time. The price store accumulates settled closes retroactively, so the sample floor is a waiting problem, not a design problem.
  • One decision from you: whether to keep publishing an empty leaderboard (honest) or to publish a "provisional, not certified" board with the gates printed on every row. It is listed in Suggestions as S01.
