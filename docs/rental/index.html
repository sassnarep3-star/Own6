Bottom line. Both units are fully rent-controlled (built 1940, pre–June 13, 1979). The only increases that survive a San Francisco tenant lawyer's review are (a) the annual allowable increase and (b) properly banked skips — computed on base rent, once every 12 months, with a Rent Increase License, a 30- or 90-day written notice, and no rounding up. Market-rate jumps mid-tenancy, roommate surcharges and retaliatory or discriminatory increases are unlawful.

What would a SF tenant lawyer check?

Must be true ✔

  • Increase ≤ AGA or petition-approved amount (S-01 ↗ · S-05 ↗)
  • Not more than once per 12 months; first increase 12+ months after lease start (S-01 ↗)
  • Banked skips only after ≥24 full months with no increase; correct portion stated in notice (S-04 ↗)
  • Rent Increase License held before service; notice states dollar, percentage, effective date (S-01 ↗)
  • 30-day notice; 90-day if combined increase >10%; +5 days if mailed (S-12 ↗)
  • Base rent only; housing services included; passthroughs excluded; no rounding up (S-01 ↗)

Red flags ✘

  • Any increase above AGA without an approved petition (S-05 ↗)
  • Surcharge for a new/additional roommate (S-01 ↗)
  • Increase coinciding with protected-class activity or retaliation (S-17 ↗)
  • Different treatment of one tenant cohort with no objective, documented basis (S-16 ↗)
  • "Market rate" reset during an ongoing tenancy (S-15 ↗)
  • Banked portion imposed before 24 months elapsed (S-04 ↗)
