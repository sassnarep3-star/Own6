Market Practices — Corporations vs Individuals

The legal framework treats corporate and individual owners identically for these units; only AB 1482's owner-identity carve-out differs in narrow cases. Practices are labeled and flagged — no official 94122 practice dataset was readable.

General practice — labeled, unverified (F-R13)

Institutional / corporate owners

  • Systematically impose the maximum lawful AGA every year on the anniversary date — no banking build-up; compliance-calendar management.
  • Maintain Housing Inventory registrations and batch filings across portfolios; dedicated counsel sign-off before notices.
  • Where banked balances are imposed in one letter, the same §827(b)(3)(A) 90-day rule applies once the combined increase exceeds 10% — corporate notices still need 90 days.
  • practice observation — no official 94122 source; see F-R13

Individual / "mom-and-pop" owners

  • Frequently skip anniversary increases (relational tenants), building large banked balances — exactly the Unit 2 pattern (11.5% banked since 2020, if never imposed).
  • Then either never impose (leaving value unrealized), or impose once with a 90-day combined notice after long silence.
  • More likely to err procedurally: license, mailing (+5 days), rounding, roommate surcharges — the SF.gov "common mistakes" list applies to them most (S-01).
  • practice observation — no official 94122 source; see F-R13

What "normal business practice" means for this property

Benchmark behavior: (1) register Housing Inventory / hold license; (2) compute AGA on base rent without rounding; (3) notice correctly (30/90 days +5 if mailed); (4) same template and method for both units; (5) document the ledger basis for any banked portion; (6) keep proof of service. An increase structured this way is what a SF tenant lawyer or the Rent Board itself would recognize as ordinary, lawful practice. Anything beyond it — percentage games, selective targeting, roommate fees — is not "normal business practice," corporate or otherwise.
