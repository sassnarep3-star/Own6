Analysis · equal-treatment posture

One Unit or Both? — Discrimination-Risk Analysis

The law does not require parity between units; it forbids protected-class-based and retaliatory treatment. The lowest-risk lawful posture is uniform AGA-based increases on both units with identical documentation — or a documented objective reason for any deviation.

Short answer. Raise both units — with the same objective, published formula applied to each unit's own lawful base, identical notice templates, and identical documentation. The law allows per-tenancy increases; it does not require parity. But uniform treatment is the only posture that never even raises the question of discrimination, mirrors normal business practice, and passes SF tenant-lawyer scrutiny cleanly. A one-unit raise is defensible only with a documented, objective reason — different banking histories — retained in the file.

Must both units get the same increase?No. Chapter 37 keys increases to each tenancy (base rent, anniversary date, banking history). Two units can lawfully receive different increases for objective reasons.SF.gov · banking ↗

Recommendation

  1. Serve both units with 30-day written notices imposing the 2026-27 AGA of 1.6% (Unit 1: $63.92 → $4,058.92; Unit 2: $50.40 → $3,200.40; Unit 2 base subject to F-R7 verification first).
  2. In parallel, assemble Unit 2's full ledger; if it confirms no increases since June 1, 2019, serve a separate 90-day notice (or a correctly phased 30-day/yr series) imposing the 11.5% banked + 1.6% annual = 13.1% ($412.65 → $3,562.65) — only after F-R7 clears and license/banked-portion itemization are confirmed.
  3. Hold Unit 1's banked 1.4% until on/after February 1, 2027 (F-R5), then impose with 30-day notice as a clearly itemized banked portion.
  4. Use one template, one methodology note, one service method, one filing for both units. Put the objective basis (anniversary dates, banking histories, form letters) in the permanent file.
  5. If any amount above AGA is contemplated (capital improvement etc.), file the petition/worksheet before noticing.
Why not maximize everything now? Unit 2's 13.1% single notice triggers the 90-day rule and invites — legitimately — a tenant challenge and free-counsel review; that is lawful only when the ledger, license and service are perfect. If anything is off, a phased approach (1.6%/yr 30-day notices, never exceeding 10%/12-month window) is slower but far safer. Speed never justifies an unlawful notice.
