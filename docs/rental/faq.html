FAQ · both units

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers with source links: the AGA, banking, notice periods, roommates, one-vs-both units, license vs petition, and what happens if tenants refuse.

Q1. What is a reasonable rent increase in SF 94122 right now?

For rent-controlled units, whatever the Rent Board allows: 1.6% for increases effective 3/1/2026–2/28/2027 (base rent only, once per 12 months). Anything above that requires an approved Rent Board petition (e.g., capital improvements). SF.gov ↗

Q2. The building is from 1940 — which rules apply?

Full SF Rent Ordinance coverage: rent-increase limits AND just-cause eviction protections. Pre–June 13, 1979 rent-controlled stock. SF.gov coverage ↗

Q3. Can AB 1482's 5%+CPI/10% cap be used instead? Like in 2024-25 when inflation was higher?

No — §1947.12(d)(3) exempts housing subject to a more-restrictive local rent control. SF's AGA always governs these units. AB 1482 is only relevant if SF coverage failed — it does not here. leginfo ↗

Q4. Did 3.6% in 2023-24 and 40% increases around 2021-22 happen in SF?

Yes — the official Form 571 shows 3.6% (3/1/23–2/29/24), 2.3% (2022-23), 0.7% (2021-22), 1.8% (2020-21), 2.6% (2019-20); the 2008-era table shows no 10%+ year since 1982's 7.0% cap. Form 571 ↗

Q5. Can we charge Unit 1's current tenants for the April 1, 2026 change (new roommate)?

No. An added roommate is never a lawful basis for added rent, even if the lease says so. SF.gov ↗

Q6. Unit 2 hasn't seen an increase since 2019. What's the maximum we can do?

If the ledger confirms no increases: 11.5% banked (sum of the six skipped AGAs, not compounded) + 1.6% annual = 13.1% on $3,150 = $412.65 ($3,562.65) — but a single notice that large requires 90 days, and the banked part must be itemized. Verify the current base first (F-R7) and confirm the license. SF.gov banking ↗

Q7. Does a banked increase expire?

Not during the tenancy; no cap on total banked; survives sale of the property (new owner may use it); does NOT carry to a new tenant. But a skip is bankable only after at least 24 full months pass with no increase. SF.gov ↗

Q8. 30-day or 90-day notice — which one?

30 days if the increase (alone or combined with any other increase in the prior 12 months) is ≤10%; 90 days if >10%. Mailed service adds 5 days. Civil Code §827(b)(2)–(3). leginfo §827 ↗

Q9. Can we raise only one unit now and the other next year?

Lawful if each unit's notice stands alone on its own anniversary/banking math. Riskier posture than uniform raises; needs a documented objective basis; see the One-vs-Both page — recommended: both units, same method. One vs both →

Q10. What must the notice include?

Dollar amount, percentage, effective date, and (for banked portions) which part is banked. Use the percentage in effect on the effective date. No rounding up. SF.gov ↗

Q11. Do we need Rent Board approval for the regular annual increase?

No — but you DO need the Rent Increase License (Housing Inventory registration) first. Only petition-based increases (capital improvement etc.) need Rent Board approval before the tenant must pay. SF.gov petitions ↗

Q12. What if tenants refuse the increase?

Do not self-help (no lockouts, no deposit grabs). The AGA increase properly noticed becomes part of the lease; nonpayment triggers the ordinary nonpayment process (3-day notice to pay or quit) — but eviction triggers Tenant Right to Counsel (free attorney) for the tenant. Talk to the Rent Board counselor first: (415) 252-4600. SF.gov evictions ↗

Q13. Is there any federal rent control or HUD cap?

No federal rent control exists. HUD FMRs (FY2027 effective 10/1/2026) benchmark subsidized programs only; they cap nothing in private tenancies. FY2027 area figures are blank/unverified here — F-R2. HUDUSER ↗

Q14. Does raising both units the same amount protect us from a discrimination claim?

It eliminates disparate-impact optics; it does not immunize discriminatory motive — but combined with objective AGA math, identical templates and documented file notes, it is the strongest practical defense. Protected classes under FEHA/Unruh/FHA make selective increases dangerous. FEHA ↗

Q15. What should happen before any notice is served?

(1) Pull Unit 2's ledger + Housing Inventory report (F-R7); (2) confirm the license; (3) verify Unit 1's banking dates (F-R5); (4) have a qualified SF attorney or Rent Board counselor review the notice; (5) serve with proof; (6) calendar the next eligible date.
